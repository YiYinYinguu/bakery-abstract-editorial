# Bakery recognition cues

Use this reference after inspecting the source. Select only two to four cues that are both visible and necessary. Never add a canonical feature that the photographed item does not have.

## Rustic and scored loaves

- Use the footprint: boule, batard, elongated loaf, braid, ring, or segmented pull-apart form.
- Preserve scoring through unequal openings, not decorative white stripes.
- When visible, express flour as a broken pale crown and baking as a restrained darker base or inner score edge.
- Prefer dry-brush masses and interrupted contours. Avoid crust pores, crumb grains, and photographic flour dust.

## Sandwich loaves, tin loaves, and buns

- Preserve the pan-defined footprint, domed crown, joined segments, or split top visible in the source.
- Use one restrained base line or side plane only when it distinguishes the form.
- Keep the mass soft and irregular rather than inflated, glossy, or toy-like.

## Rolls and spirals

- Preserve the inward-turning path, layer count, and outer boundary.
- Use arcs, broken bands, and muted filling colors.
- Let small gaps separate layers; avoid rendering photographic crumb, glaze, or cream texture.

## Croissants and laminated pastries

- Preserve the crescent, straight roll, fan, or folded footprint visible in the source.
- Suggest lamination with a few broken nested bands or directional folds, not many realistic flakes.
- Keep tips, shoulders, and center unequal. Avoid a polished crescent icon or glossy 3D rendering.

## Layered cakes and pastries

- Preserve the layer rhythm, one decisive top shape, and one filling contrast.
- Use stacked bands or offset masses with slight irregularity.
- Avoid perfect rectangles, detailed crumbs, frosting highlights, and decorative garnish unless essential to identity.

## Tarts and pies

- Preserve the outer rim, open filling field, wedge, lattice, or folded edge visible in the source.
- Use nested fields, broken arcs, or restrained crossings rather than realistic fruit or filling texture.
- Keep the crust boundary irregular and incomplete; avoid a perfect circular badge.

## Cookies, biscuits, and scones

- Preserve the count only when explicitly requested; otherwise select one representative item.
- Use the decisive footprint, split, stamped edge, or inclusion pattern as one restrained cue.
- Avoid repeating dots, chips, or seeds as decoration and avoid cute icon proportions.

## Steamed and Asian dough items

- Preserve the construction visible in the source: smooth dome, pleated closure, rolled spiral, folded layer, braid, stamped top, or exposed filling boundary.
- For mantou and bao, favor soft broken masses and restrained seam cues rather than baked crust language.
- For mooncakes and egg-yolk pastries, retain one decisive shell, layer, cut-face, or filling relationship without turning it into a decorative badge.
- Avoid invented steam, baskets, wrappers, ingredient scatter, and culturally generic ornament.

## Ring breads and bagels

- Preserve the negative space and asymmetry of the ring.
- Use one broken loop or several connected brush segments.
- Avoid a perfect torus, smooth icon geometry, and realistic blistered crust.

## Cut faces and filled baked goods

- Treat the outer bake and filling as two or three nested color roles.
- Preserve the filling boundary, not ingredient inventory.
- Use a few irregular inclusions only when they are a decisive visible cue.
- A single item split into two connected or paired halves may count as one subject; otherwise prefer one half.

## Multiple baked goods in the source

- Choose one representative item based on visibility, distinctiveness, and composition.
- Do not average different products into a fictional hybrid.
- Do not reproduce the whole tray merely because it is present in the photograph.

## Failure diagnosis

If the result is unrecognizable, add or strengthen exactly one construction cue: a score edge, laminated fold, spiral path, layer boundary, tart rim, ring void, or filling field.

If the result looks like an icon, break the contour, vary spacing, expose brush endings, and remove symmetry.

If the result is too realistic, remove surface texture before removing structural identity.

If the result is cute, reduce roundness, facial association, saturated colors, and toy-like volume.
